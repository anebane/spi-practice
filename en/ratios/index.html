Ratio and share questions

Numerical reasoning practice > Ratio and share questions

Ratio questions are short and are failed for one reason: putting the wrong value on the bottom. The wording tells you which, and the wording is easy to skim past.

On this page

  2. Worked example
  3. Share of a total
  4. Ratios given as parts

1. Which value goes on the bottom

"A is how many times B" means A ÷ B. The value after "times" is the denominator.

  • "The revenue of D is how many times that of C" → D ÷ C
  • "C is what fraction of D" → C ÷ D
  • "The ratio of D to C" → D : C, which is D ÷ C expressed as a ratio

Getting this backwards produces the reciprocal, and the reciprocal is always one of the options.

2. Worked example: how many times

The table below shows the annual revenue of each store.

Store A 440   Store D 630   Store B 610   Store C 180
(unit: £000s)

The revenue of Store D is how many times that of Store C?

How to work it

  • "D is how many times C" → D on top, C on the bottom
  • 630 ÷ 180
  • Both divide by 90: 7 ÷ 2 = 3.5 times

Simplifying before dividing is faster than reaching for long division, and it is the step that makes these doable in the time allowed.

3. Share of a total

A share question needs a denominator you have to build yourself: the total of every row.

  • Add all rows to get the total
  • Divide the row in question by that total
  • Multiply by 100 if a percentage is asked for

The common error is dividing by the largest row instead of the total. If the options include a number above 100%, that error is what the option is there to catch.

4. Ratios given as parts

When a ratio is given as "3 : 5", the total is eight parts, not five. To split £4,000 in the ratio 3 : 5:

  • Total parts: 3 + 5 = 8
  • One part: 4,000 ÷ 8 = 500
  • The shares are 1,500 and 2,500

Always find the value of one part first. Trying to go straight to the answer is where the arithmetic goes wrong.
